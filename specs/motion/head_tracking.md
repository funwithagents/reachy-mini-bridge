# Head tracking (`head_tracking.py`)

**Status:** Implemented

## Purpose

The bridge's own head tracker: it takes the target face that user perception reports ([user_perception.md](../vision/user_perception.md)) and turns it into an **aim** — the head pose that looks at that person — which the motion loop composes into the idle move ([motion.md](motion.md) "The gaze layer"), so the robot looks at the person it is talking to while it keeps breathing, and idles in full again once nobody is there.

Upstream tracks daemon-side, with the client able only to switch it on and off: at full weight the daemon discards the client's head target, on a lost face it recentres and then keeps discarding, and in the sim its aim is wrong ([../docs/internals/upstream-sdk-notes.md](../../docs/internals/upstream-sdk-notes.md) "Face tracking"). Owning the aim in the bridge removes the attention loop, `play_emotion`'s weight dip and two sim-launcher corrections that worked around it, and is what lets a developer's own detector ([user_perception.md](../vision/user_perception.md) "Custom detectors") steer the head. The tracker sits between perception and motion: geometry on one side, gaze policy on the other, and no knowledge of where the faces come from.

## Core concepts / Decided

### Inputs and outputs

- **In:** every poll of the detection loop — an observation (every `FaceReport`, not the debounced count events) through `observe(report)`, a poll without one (no new frame yet, a failed `detect`) through `tick()`, so the tracker keeps time whether or not frames arrive; the motion loop's history of the head poses the robot reported, through `head_pose_history()` (the times and poses, for the delay estimate to index); the camera model of the active camera (below); the caller's `TrackingSettings` ("Configuration and verbs").
- **Out:** `motion.set_gaze(aim, focus=…)` — an aim, `GazeAim(look, roll, offset)`: the look-at pose, the mirrored roll and the posture's offset ("The posture" below), which the loop composes — or `None` to withdraw it, and whether the head holds exactly on it. The tracker never touches the robot; the loop is the one writer.
- **Out, for callers:** its state as an observable, `bridge.head_tracking` ("The head tracking report" below) — which face the head follows, by `track_id`, and the attention it derives.
- It runs whenever tracking is on (`motion.tracking` at entry, `start_head_tracking` / `stop_head_tracking` while entered), built once per session by the bridge.

### Whom the head follows

The face report lists everyone in view, each under a `track_id` that stays the same while the detection loop keeps seeing that person ([user_perception.md](../vision/user_perception.md) "Tracks"); the tracker chooses one to follow and holds on to it — the robot looks at a person, not at whichever face is largest on a given frame. On every observation:

1. **Following, and the face is reported.** The tracker keeps following its `track_id` and aims that face, whatever other faces appear — a larger one included.
2. **Following, and the face is missing.** The previous aim stands: the head keeps looking where the face was last seen (the aim is a head pose in the world frame, so the head holds that direction while the idle move and the body stay free). The face coming back — under the same `track_id`, within the detection loop's miss window — is followed again at once, as if it had never left.
3. **Switching.** Once the followed face has been missing for `TRACKING_SWITCH_S = 1.0` s, the tracker switches to the **biggest** face in view (largest `size`) of at least `TRACKING_MIN_SIZE = 0.07` of the frame's height — about upstream's acquisition gate, 0.3 % of a 16:9 frame's area for a square face. With none that large, it keeps holding and switches to the first one that shows, until the loss below.
4. **Acquiring.** Following nobody — tracking just started, or after a loss — the tracker follows the biggest face of at least `TRACKING_MIN_SIZE` on the first observation that shows one, at once.

The minimum size gates switching and acquiring only: a followed face moving away stays followed while it is detected. The wait before a switch is what keeps a detector's flicker, a turned head or a hand in front of a face from sending the head to someone else; the hold toward the last position is what makes that wait look like attention rather than a freeze. The times are in seconds of the tracker's clock, so they hold whatever the detection rate (`target_fps`).

### The aim

- **Geometry.** Upstream's, with the bridge's inputs: the normalised face becomes a pixel of a nominal frame, undistorted through the intrinsics into a ray in the camera frame, rotated into the world by the **head pose at the time of the observation**, offset by the head-to-camera transform, and turned into a look-at pose (`reachy_mini.vision.look_at.look_at_image_pose`, `default_head_to_camera_transform`). A `look_at_image_pose` that raises (a face at the frame's edge under strong distortion) is one `DEBUG` line and the previous aim stands.
- **Intrinsics — the camera model.** `CameraModel(K, D, size, fixed)`. On a robot, the SDK client's calibrated matrix and distortion (`robot.media.camera.camera_specs.K` / `D`, at that camera's calibrated resolution; the Lite's specs when the client runs without media). In the sim, upstream's matrix is wrong for the rendered camera ([sim_daemon.md](../daemon/sim_daemon.md) open question 2), so the model is the bridge's own ideal pinhole — `pinhole_intrinsics` / `sim_hfov_deg`, moved out of the sim launcher into this module — of the scene's eye camera (`SIM_EYE_CAMERA_FOVY_DEG = 80` at 16:9, pinned against the MJCF by a sim-extra test) for a `sim` camera source, or of the reported `hfov_deg` for a `webcam`, chosen by the sim's camera ("The sim's camera" below).
- **The sim's camera.** The daemon runs the camera, so the model follows what it reports: `bridge.daemon.camera` ([daemon.md](../daemon/daemon.md) "What the daemon reports"), read before the tracker is built, and a contradicting config gets its warning there. A daemon that reports none — upstream's `reachy-mini-daemon --sim`, an older launcher, a failed read — leaves `DaemonConfig.camera` to stand in. The camera model holds for the session, and the face markers read the same one ([sim_displays.md](../daemon/sim_displays.md)). The `real` backend keeps the client's calibration.
- **A fixed camera.** With a `webcam` source the camera does not turn with the head, so the ray is rotated by the **neutral** pose instead of the head's: the aim is an absolute direction from the rest pose, and the head eases to it and holds — step aside and it follows by the same angle, stand still and it stays. Head-mounted geometry would feed the head's own motion back into the aim and run it to its limit within a second. (This was the sim launcher's third correction; it belongs here.)
- **The head pose the frame was taken from.** A face is seen in a frame taken some time before the tracker receives it, from wherever the head was then; aiming it against a more recent head pose adds the head's motion since the frame a second time, and the head overshoots — with the loop's easing, it hunts back and forth around the face. On the live backends a report's `ts` is the frame's **arrival** time at the bridge ([camera.md](../vision/camera.md) "The frame's time and the head pose": upstream's frames carry no capture time), which is later than the capture by the render or sensor readout, the stream and the decode, and the actual head lags the commanded one by the IK and the motors. None of that is reported, and it differs between the sim, a robot and a webcam. So for a report without a pose the tracker **aims against the actual head pose at `t_obs − L`, and estimates the delay `L` online**:
  - *The actual pose.* The motion loop records the head pose the robot reports — the SDK's `get_current_head_pose()`, which the daemon publishes on every control tick — over the last `GAZE_HISTORY_S = 4.0` s, whether or not it is commanding, and answers `head_pose_at(t)` for a time on the monotonic clock and `head_pose_history()` with all of it ([motion.md](motion.md) "A history of head poses"). The robot's own actuation lag is then measured, not assumed.
  - *The observation's time* `t_obs` is the report's `ts`: the frame's time on the bridge's own monotonic clock ([camera.md](../vision/camera.md)), whatever host the daemon runs on. Only a new detection counts: the detection loop reports once per new frame, so a report is new when its `ts` differs from the last one.
  - *The delay estimate.* While a person stays put, the world direction of the face — the ray of each detection rotated by the head pose at `t_obs − L` — is the same for every detection only at the true `L`; any other `L` mixes in the head's own motion. The tracker keeps the new detections of the last `DELAY_WINDOW_S = 3.0` s, scores every `L` in `[0, DELAY_MAX_S = 0.5]` by the spread of those directions (one minus the length of their mean unit vector) and moves its estimate `DELAY_SMOOTHING = 0.3` of the way toward the best one, on each new detection. The fit is informative only while the head turns and only when some delay explains the directions: it runs when the head pose over the window spans at least `DELAY_MIN_MOTION_DEG = 4.0`, and it is taken only when it is **distinct** — the best delay's spread at most `DELAY_MAX_CONTRAST = 0.5` of the worst's. A person moving while the head turns spreads the directions at every `L` alike and flattens the score; a flat score's minimum is noise, and taking it would run the estimate away toward the range's end (measured on the viewer sim while the portrait glides sideways: the estimate climbed to the cap and the aim then lagged the head, the approach creeping onto the face) — so the estimate holds through it. Tracking a face turns the head, so the estimate is learned on the first approach and kept up to date by every later one; it starts at `DELAY_PRIOR_S = 0.2`, a starting point the first turn corrects, not a value the tracker relies on. On the viewer sim, against the frame's arrival time, the estimate settles between 0.05 and 0.45 s (mostly 0.1–0.25; eight tracked turns over four runs, the head on the face each time); the range's cap is what an arrival-stamped frame can lag by on a local daemon (render or sensor readout, stream, decode, the motors' lag). `delay_s` reads the current estimate.
  - *A fixed camera* needs no estimate: its ray is rotated by the neutral pose, whatever the head did.
  - *Observations that carry their own pose.* A detection that arrives with the head pose it was captured from — a report whose frame the camera feed stamped with its capture time and the pose at that time ([camera.md](../vision/camera.md) "The frame's time and the head pose"; [user_perception.md](../vision/user_perception.md) "The face report") — is aimed against that pose directly, with no estimate: exact whatever the latency. The feed attaches a pose only to a capture time, so a pose on a report is one the tracker can trust. The estimate is the mechanism for frames that carry only an arrival time — every live backend's today, and none the day upstream's frames carry a capture time (camera.md open question 1).
  - *One rule, shared.* The three cases above are one function, `frame_head_pose(report, camera, history, delay, now)`: the neutral pose for a fixed camera, the report's own `head_pose` when stamped, else the history's pose at the report's time (`ts`, or `now` when it is unset) minus `delay`. The aim calls it with the estimate, after the new detection has joined the fit. The sim's face markers call it with `delay_s` to place a face where the tracker sees it ([sim_displays.md](../daemon/sim_displays.md) "The head pose of a frame"). It reads and never feeds the estimate.

### Easing, loss, focus

- **Easing** is the loop's: the tracker publishes a new aim per observation with a face, and the loop eases toward it per tick ([motion.md](motion.md) `GAZE_ALPHA`), so an aim arriving at the detector's rate — 10 a second, upstream's camera-feed cap — never steps the head.
- **Loss.** The followed face is missing from the last observation that showed it — whether the observations since lacked it or none arrived at all (no camera frame, a detector that keeps failing). After `TRACKING_LOST_S = 2.0` s of that (upstream's own lost-face timeout) with no face large enough to switch to — on an observation or on a tick — the tracker follows nobody and withdraws the aim (`None`) and the loop fades the gaze layer out — the head eases back onto the idle move, at or near neutral, and the robot idles in full. The next face large enough is acquired, publishes an aim again and the layer fades back in. The grace the attention loop used to keep (3 s, longer than the daemon's recentre) has no reason left: there is no daemon recentre to wait out.
- **Focus.** `start_head_tracking(focus=…)`'s flag, held by the tracker and handed to the loop with every aim. Off (the default), the head is the aim with the idle move's own gaze-time motion composed on top (its `gaze_offsets`, [motion.md](motion.md) "The gaze layer" — breathing keeps its breath and antennas and tones its roaming down; a custom move that defines none is still on the aim). On, the head holds exactly on the aim — the idle move's head motion is left out, its antennas kept, so the robot stares but still looks alive. There is no blend weight: the daemon's tracker blended its aim with the client's target by one, which the bridge's gaze layer has no use for (the aim is always composed in full, faded in and out over `GAZE_FADE_S`). `stop_head_tracking()` withdraws the aim.
- **`attention` is derived**, no longer a loop of its own: `"engaged"` while the tracker follows a face (its aim held, the face reported or missing for less than the loss time), `"watching"` while tracking is on and it follows nobody, `None` when tracking is off ([bridge.md](../core/bridge.md) "Attention / gaze").

### The posture: mirroring, approach, personal space

Where the head looks is the aim's direction; how it stands toward the person is the rest of the aim. Three behaviours, each switched by its own `enabled` in `TrackingSettings` ("Configuration and verbs"), shape it while the tracker follows a face:

- **Mirroring** — the head rolls with the person's head roll, as a mirror does: the robot's eye line is set parallel to the person's, so both heads lean toward the same side of the room.
- **Approach** — the head sits `lean_mm` forward of the look-at pose: the robot comes a little toward the person it attends to.
- **Personal space** — a person nearer than `distance_m` pushes the head back, the retreat growing over `span_m` of intrusion up to `max_back_mm`.

**Carried in the aim.** The aim's parts compose to the look-at pose (a rotation, upstream's `look_at_image_pose`), rolled about its own viewing axis by the mirrored roll, and translated along the **horizontal projection of its viewing direction** by the offset — the approach's lean minus the personal space's retreat — so a person at 60° of yaw is leaned toward, not the front of the robot. The motion loop composes it as it composes the direction ([motion.md](motion.md) "The gaze layer"): eased by `GAZE_ALPHA`, faded in and out over `GAZE_FADE_S`, left out of primaries — an emotion plays as recorded and the re-entry blends back onto the aim, roll and lean included — with the idle move's `gaze_offsets` composed on top. **Focus** holds the head exactly on that aim: the posture is part of it, only the idle move's own head motion is left out.

**A posture, not a motion.** The loop's easing (a time constant of about 0.1 s) is right for following a moving face and wrong for a posture: a roll that followed every eye-line wobble, a lean that pumped as the size flickers, would be noise. The tracker filters each component on its own clock before it builds the aim — a first-order filter of `ROLL_SMOOTHING_S = 0.3` s on the roll and `OFFSET_SMOOTHING_S = 0.6` s on the offset — fast enough to follow a person's tilt within a second, slow enough that the deadband's leftover jitter stays at a tenth of a degree — stepped on every aimed observation by the time since the previous one. The components **freeze** while the followed face is missing (the hold toward its last position keeps its posture), carry over a switch to another person (the posture eases from where it stands), and **reset** to zero when the aim is withdrawn, so the next acquisition grows the posture in from the look-at alone. A settings change while tracking runs moves the filters' targets, never their values: a behaviour switched off eases out over its time constant, one switched on eases in — no step, whatever the call.

**The mirrored roll.**
- *In the world, against the frame's head pose.* The camera turns with the head, so the roll a face shows in the image is the person's roll less the robot's own roll when the frame was taken — breathing's roaming, and the mirrored roll itself. Read as an image angle, the mirror would feed back on itself (the head rolls to mirror, the face then looks less rolled, the head unrolls). The tracker lifts the face's eye line — the image direction at angle `roll` (y down) — into the camera frame, rotates it into the world by `frame_head_pose` (the same pose the direction is aimed against; the neutral pose for a fixed camera), projects it onto the plane across the look-at's viewing axis, and measures its angle from the look-at's own lateral axis about that viewing axis: that angle is the person's roll as seen from where the head will look, independent of what the head does.
- *Deadband, gain, clamp.* The roll is the angle of the line through two landmarks; on a desk-range face at the default detection width the eyes are about 50 px apart, one pixel of landmark error is about a degree, and landmarks wander by a pixel or two from frame to frame on a still face — an upright person reads ±1–3° that the filter would turn into a head never quite level. A soft deadband of `ROLL_DEADBAND_DEG = 3.0` (the magnitude less the deadband, continuous through zero, so a tilt at the threshold never flickers) makes an upright person read exactly zero; then `gain` (default `1.0`), then the clamp `±max_deg` (default `20°`, between breathing's roaming and the emotions' ±30°). A face without a roll (a detector that gives neither eyes nor an orientation) has a mirrored roll of zero.
- *The roll's source.* The mirror is as good as the detector's roll. YuNet's eye-line roll depends on the face's size **in the detected image** ([user_perception.md](../vision/user_perception.md) "The shipped detector"): usable from about 120 px of face, right from about 200 px — on the Lite at the default detection width of 640, a face at 0.45 m reads about two thirds of its roll, nearer more, farther less. A detector that fits a head gives the true roll. One that under-reads by a fraction `f` still mirrors most of a tilt, because each reading adds the head's own roll back exactly: the person's roll is read as `f · (true − head) + head`, so the head settles where `head = gain · (reading − deadband)` holds — at gain 1, `true − deadband / f`, short of the true roll by the deadband over the fraction rather than by the fraction itself, and stable since each step closes `f` of the gap. `gain` stays the user's correction; at 1 it overshoots nothing whatever the detector's fraction. Measured on the viewer sim (2026-11-03): a portrait rolled 25° at 0.28 m, YuNet at 640 reading about a third of it from a level head (sim_scene.md "Geometry"), mirrored to the clamp.

**The offset.**
- `offset_mm = lean_mm − retreat_mm(d)`, each term present while its behaviour is enabled, with `d` the followed face's `distance_m` ([user_perception.md](../vision/user_perception.md) "The face report"):

  ```
  retreat_mm(d) = 0                                                 d ≥ distance_m
                = max_back_mm · min(1, (distance_m − d) / span_m)   nearer
  ```

- The curve is continuous, so a person standing at `distance_m` gets a retreat near zero and no threshold to pump across; the filter absorbs the distance's frame-to-frame noise. A face without a distance (no camera model, a detector without a size) gets the lean alone.
- The head's travel (some 30 mm, what the emotions library's recordings span forward and back: `reprimand1` +30 mm, `go_away1` −30 mm) cannot *keep* a distance against a person moving by tens of centimetres: the retreat is a gesture — a recoil that reads as "you are close" — which is why its whole range is spent over a short span (default `span_m = 0.20`). The defaults: `lean_mm = 15` (three breaths' amplitude, in another axis, below every emotion's forward lean), `distance_m = 0.40`, `max_back_mm = 20` over `span_m = 0.20` — a gradual give-way rather than a recoil, tuned with a person in front of a Lite (2026-11-03) from first values of 12 mm, 25 mm over 0.10 m and a 12° roll cap.
- Nobody followed, no offset: the aim is withdrawn and the head is on its idle move at neutral.

**Within reach.** Not every aim is a pose the robot can take: the Stewart platform's workspace shrinks when a retreat, a roll and a turn add up, and a pose the daemon's inverse kinematics cannot solve is refused — upstream logs `IK error: WARNING: Collision detected or head pose not achievable!` and keeps the previous joint targets, so the head freezes on the last pose it reached and the tracking reads as stuck at the far sides. Measured 2026-11-03 with upstream's analytical solver at the loop's body yaw (0): a full 25 mm retreat is refused from 56° of yaw level, from 38° with a 12° roll once breathing's motion is on top, from 22° with the head also looking up 15°, and from 18° with a 25° roll and a breath of lift; a forward lean with the same rolls stays reachable almost everywhere; the yaw alone never is the limit (past 65° the solver turns the body itself). The tracker does not check this itself: it hands the loop the aim's **parts** — the look-at, the filtered roll, the filtered offset (`GazeAim`) — and the loop, the one place that knows the exact pose it sends, composes them with the idle move's motion and gives up, as the kinematics require, the idle motion first, then the offset, then the roll, then part of the direction ([motion.md](motion.md) "Within reach"): looking at the person comes first, the stance after. `HeadTrackingReport.roll_deg` and `offset_mm` publish the posture the tracker asks for; what the loop gives up is not published.

### The personal space event

A client often wants to react when the person it talks to comes close — step back in speech as the head does, lower its voice, say "a bit close!" — so the tracker publishes it, on the report a client already reads to know whom the head follows: `HeadTrackingReport.in_personal_space`, a published change when it flips.

- **Whose:** the followed person's, by the face's `distance_m` ([user_perception.md](../vision/user_perception.md) "The face report") — the person the head attends to, the one the retreat would step back from. Anyone else in view has a distance on `bridge.faces` for a client to judge itself.
- **The zone** is `personal_space.distance_m` ("Configuration and verbs"). The event is reported whether or not the retreat is enabled: a client may want to know without the head stepping back.
- **The boundary.** Entered nearer than `distance_m`; left only beyond `distance_m + exit_margin_m`, so a person standing at the boundary never flickers the event. The distance wanders by about ±5 % of itself from frame to frame (±2 cm at 0.4 m, ±4 cm at 0.8 m), so the margin defaults to a share of the distance, `PERSONAL_SPACE_EXIT_SHARE = 0.125` of `distance_m` — 5 cm at the default 0.40 m, 10 cm at 0.8 m — and a config sets it in metres for a detector whose box is jumpier. Judged on every observation that shows the followed face; held while the face is missing (the switch and loss holds), like the posture.
- **Reset with the person.** `False` when the tracker follows nobody (the loss, `stop()`), and judged afresh when it switches to someone else — their own entry, not the margin earned by the one before. A face without a distance (no camera model) is outside.

### The head tracking report

What the head is doing about the people in front of it is the tracker's state, not the detection's: the face report says who is there ([user_perception.md](../vision/user_perception.md)), this report says whom the head follows. The tracker publishes it as an observable ([observable.md](../core/observable.md)), `bridge.head_tracking: Observable[HeadTrackingReport]`, defined in `head_tracking.py`:

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class HeadTrackingReport:
    active: bool            # the tracker is running (tracking on, inside a session)
    focus: bool             # it holds the head exactly on the face (start_head_tracking's focus)
    attention: str | None   # "engaged" | "watching" | None — the derived state above
    track_id: int | None    # the track of the face the head follows; None unless engaged
    ts: float               # the time of the face report behind the last aim; 0.0 before any
    roll_deg: float = 0.0   # the mirrored roll in the last aim, after the filter ("The posture")
    offset_mm: float = 0.0  # the posture's offset in the last aim: + forward, − back
    in_personal_space: bool = False  # the followed person is inside the personal space (below)
```

- **`track_id`** is the `track_id` of the face the tracker follows ("Whom the head follows"). It stays through the followed face's absence while the head holds toward its last position, changes when the tracker switches, and is `None` once attention is `"watching"` or tracking is off. A client that recognises people joins its identities to the face report by `track_id` ([user_perception.md](../vision/user_perception.md) "The face report") and reads here which of them the head follows.
- **Published changes.** The tracker `set`s the report when any of `active`, `focus`, `attention`, `track_id` or `in_personal_space` changes — so `async for report in bridge.head_tracking.changes()` wakes on *tracking started / stopped*, *focus switched*, *the head engaged someone / handed the head back* (on an observation or on a tick, "Easing, loss, focus"), *the head now follows someone else*, *the followed person entered / left the personal space* — and `update`s it on every other aimed observation (a fresh `ts`, the posture's `roll_deg` and `offset_mm`), never waking a subscriber on a face moving or the posture easing.
- **Outside a session**, and while tracking is off, the value is `HeadTrackingReport(active=False, focus=False, attention=None, track_id=None, ts=0.0)` (no posture), reset through `set` at stop so a subscriber learns tracking ended; the observable itself outlives the session, as `bridge.faces` does. The bridge's `tracking_focus` and `attention` properties read the same state; `tracking` stays the settings' record (the config's value outside a session).

### Configuration and verbs

- **One settings object.** `TrackingSettings` ([config.md](../core/config.md) "`motion.tracking`") is the whole tracking behaviour: `enabled` (whether the tracker runs), `focus`, and one sub-object per posture behaviour — `mirroring` (`MirroringSettings`: `enabled`, `gain`, `max_deg`), `approach` (`ApproachSettings`: `enabled`, `lean_mm`), `personal_space` (`PersonalSpaceSettings`: `enabled`, `distance_m`, `max_back_mm`, `span_m`) — frozen dataclasses, so a caller changes one field with `dataclasses.replace`. `motion.tracking` in the config is that object (default: off, focus off, the three behaviours on with the defaults above); it lives in the `motion` block because the gaze is a layer of the motion loop.
- **Verbs.** `bridge.tracking` reads the settings in force — the bridge's own record, the config's outside a session. `set_tracking(settings)` applies a whole object while entered: `enabled` starts or stops the tracker (and the detection loop with it), every other field takes effect on the next aim through the filters, never as a step. `start_head_tracking(*, focus=None)` and `stop_head_tracking()` are its two common cases — `enabled` set on with `focus` changed when given, and `enabled` set off — so a caller switching tracking keeps the posture it configured ([bridge.md](../core/bridge.md) "Attention / gaze"). The tracker holds the settings object it is handed (`start(settings)`, `configure(settings)`), reading them on every observation.
- **Tracking implies detection, and needs a detector.** The tracker is a client of the detection loop ([user_perception.md](../vision/user_perception.md) "The detection loop"), which runs while either `face_detection.enabled` or tracking is on; starting tracking starts detection if it is not already running. With no detector configured (`face_detection.detector` `null`) tracking cannot run: `motion.tracking.enabled: true` in the config is a `ConfigError`, `start_head_tracking()` (or `set_tracking` with `enabled`) a `ValueError`.
- **A mode, not a move.** Tracking moves the head only through the motion loop, which is paused without motors and emits nothing with presence off, so `start_head_tracking` / `set_tracking` are mode switches like `set_presence`: they need no motors and raises nothing about them ([bridge.md](../core/bridge.md) "Motors" — this changes the verb's former contract, which also took a weight).
- **Detectors are not the tracker's concern.** Which detector produced a report — the shipped YuNet or a developer's ([user_perception.md](../vision/user_perception.md) "Detectors") — the tracker neither knows nor cares: the head is steered from the reports alone. The daemon's own tracking is never armed ([robot.md](../core/robot.md)).

### Lifecycle

Built by `ReachyMiniBridge.start()` for every session, right before the detection loop, and started — `start(settings)`, which arms it to act on the reports it is fed — when `motion.tracking.enabled` is on, whether or not motors are enabled, or later by `start_head_tracking()`. The bridge constructs the `MotionSession` object before them (construction starts no thread), so the tracker holds the session's `set_gaze` and `head_pose_history` from the outset; the tracker then starts with the detection loop, before the session's `start()` — an aim it hands over meanwhile waits in the session's command queue and takes effect at the first tick ([motion.md](motion.md) "The gaze layer") — and the aim shows the moment the loop is commanding. Stopped with the detection loop right after the motion session on exit ([bridge.md](../core/bridge.md) "Lifecycle"). `stop_head_tracking()` stops it mid-session and stops the detection loop too when `face_detection` is off. Between `start()` and `stop()` it runs by being fed the loop's reports; `stop()` withdraws the aim, resets the posture and leaves the object in place for the next `start()`.

### `fake` backend support

Nothing tracker-specific is needed on the fake beyond what perception and motion provide ([user_perception.md](../vision/user_perception.md) "`fake` backend support": a stub detector registered through the `custom` path, showing and hiding a face on the fake's frames; [motion.md](motion.md): recorded `targets`) and a `media.camera.camera_specs` stand-in carrying a Lite-like `K` / `D`, so `CameraModel.for_robot` runs offline. The whole pipeline then runs on the fake at real time — through the exact-pose path, since the fake's frames carry their capture pose: `tests/` observe the recorded head targets turning toward a face shown at `x = 0.5` and easing back once it is hidden for the loss timeout, an emotion playing through unchanged with a face shown and the head returning to it afterwards, and `attention` moving through `watching` → `engaged` → `watching` → `None` — each step published on `bridge.head_tracking` with the followed face's `track_id` while engaged, and none while a face merely moves. The choice is pinned the same way, with a stub scripting faces: the biggest face acquired; a bigger face appearing beside the followed one leaving the head where it is; the followed face hidden for less than `TRACKING_SWITCH_S` with another in view — the recorded targets holding toward its last position, the same `track_id` followed when it returns; hidden longer — one `set` naming the biggest remaining face's `track_id` and the head turning to it; a face below `TRACKING_MIN_SIZE` never acquired; the followed face alone and hidden past `TRACKING_LOST_S` — the aim withdrawn, `"watching"`. The sim's camera is pinned through a session on a stubbed daemon port: the daemon's camera winning, the config's standing in, and a config saying nothing on a `webcam` daemon, where the head settles at the angle that webcam sees the face at. The geometry itself is pinned offline with no daemon: a closed loop that projects the test scene's portrait through the sim camera's pinhole from the head pose, feeds the tracker, and asserts the head converges within 3° of `atan2(y, x)` with at most one bounded overshoot — with a frame taken 0.3 s before its `ts` says, whose delay the tracker must learn (the estimate converging near 0.3 s), and with a fixed camera that must settle rather than run away. The fake reports the pose it was last commanded, so its actual head has no lag; the offline loop models the frame's delay instead. The posture is pinned on the fake the same way: a face shown with a roll → the recorded targets' roll settling at the mirrored, clamped value without a step, a roll under the deadband → none; a stub whose image roll follows the fake's own head roll (the person upright, the robot rolled) → the head settling level, no feedback; a large face → the targets' head retreating along the look direction, a small one → leaning forward, a face at 30° of yaw → the lean toward it; the posture gone once the face is lost; an emotion playing through unchanged and the posture back after it; focus holding exactly on the aim, posture included; each `enabled` switched off mid-track easing its component out, never stepping; `HeadTrackingReport.roll_deg` / `offset_mm` following the filters. The personal space event: entering below `distance_m` and leaving only beyond the margin — the default share of the distance at 0.40 m and at 0.80 m, and a configured one — each one published change, a person hovering inside the margin changing nothing; reported with the retreat disabled; reset by the loss and judged afresh for a new person; a face without a distance outside.

- **[bridge.md](../core/bridge.md):** the tracking verbs, `attention` and the `head_tracking` observable, re-based on the tracker; the attention loop and `play_emotion`'s daemon-weight dip are gone.

- **[sim_daemon.md](../daemon/sim_daemon.md):** the launcher carries no tracking geometry and reports its camera ([daemon.md](../daemon/daemon.md) "What the daemon reports"); the sim camera's pinhole and the fixed-camera rule for a webcam live here.

## Open questions

1. **The webcam's cropped field of view.** The sim launcher's relay narrows a wide camera's field of view when it crops to 16:9 and knows the negotiated width only once frames flow, which can be after the bridge has built its tracker; the tracker uses the camera's own `hfov_deg`. An `effective_hfov_deg` in the daemon's report would close the gap, with a retry or a tracker that accepts a new camera model; deferred until a camera's aim error is measured to matter.
2. **Webcam calibration.** An ideal pinhole from `hfov_deg` follows a person by eye; a calibrated matrix and distortion (upstream ships a calibration tool for the robot's camera) would make the aim precise. Deferred until a manual test needs better than a few degrees.
3. **Numbers.** `TRACKING_SWITCH_S`, `TRACKING_MIN_SIZE`, `TRACKING_LOST_S`, the delay estimate's window, range, motion threshold, contrast and smoothing here, and `GAZE_ALPHA` in the loop are tuned on the viewer sim (2026-09-29: the estimate settles between 0.05 and 0.45 s against the frame's arrival time, mostly 0.1–0.25, so the 0.2 s prior stands; the range's cap and the contrast rule come from the same measurement). The posture's defaults (`lean_mm`, `max_deg`, `distance_m`, `max_back_mm`, `span_m`), `ROLL_DEADBAND_DEG` and the two smoothing time constants are set from the emotions library's ranges and YuNet's measured roll, the smoothing shortened after a first test with a person found the mirror slow (2026-11-03: a 25° tilt mirrored to 63% in 1.1 s of commanded roll against 1.85 s at 1.0 s of smoothing; a 14° tilt still takes about 2 s, the loop closing only the share of each gap the detector reads, "The mirrored roll"). All are still to be judged on a robot with a person in front of it.
5. **Frames that carry their capture time.** The exact aim for every backend is upstream's to enable: a capture timestamp on the client's frames ([camera.md](../vision/camera.md) open question 1; an upstream report is drafted, to be filed); the feed then stamps every frame with its pose and the delay estimate becomes dormant, with no change here.
4. **A bearing for the report.** Once the tracker's camera model is shared with the face report, `Face` can carry yaw / pitch in degrees ([user_perception.md](../vision/user_perception.md) open question 2).
6. **The base toward the person.** Turning the base with the head is what frees the workspace most ("Within reach": with the base following the head, every measured posture is reachable past 120° of yaw). It needs a heading the loop composes onto every move — the idle move and the emotions played toward the person, the heading held after they leave and eased back to the front — because following the person with the base alone would turn it back to the front for every emotion's blend and out again after it. Deferred to that design, in [motion.md](motion.md).
