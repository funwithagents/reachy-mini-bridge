# Motion loop, presence & breathing (`motion.py`)

**Status:** Stable

## Purpose

Everything the bridge does to keep the robot **continuously alive**: the single control loop that owns the robot's target pose, the arbitration that decides which move plays when, and the idle behaviour — **breathing**, a still neutral hold, or a caller's own idle move — that fills the gaps between verbs. It is the motion counterpart of [audio.md](../audio/audio.md): the bridge's verbs (`play_emotion`, and later the manual movement verbs) are thin over what this layer runs.

This concept exists because the robot only looks alive when *one* writer drives its target pose at a steady rate, and everything that should move the head — an emotion, a return to neutral, breathing — is composed into that one stream rather than fighting for it. Upstream gives the building blocks (the `Move` interface, its `GotoMove` interpolation, the recorded-move library, and the daemon-side face tracking and head wobbling that compose *on top of* a target pose) but no arbitration and no idle behaviour; those are what this layer adds.

### Composition model

Game-animation-style blending, split across two places:

```
   ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  MOTION LOOP (bridge, one thread, 60 Hz)                │
   │  one primary move at a time, from a FIFO queue:         │
   │    emotion (later: goto / gesture)  → else idle move    │
   │    idle = breathing | hold | custom move | nothing      │
   │  every transition is a short blend (minjerk GotoMove)   │
   │  + gaze layer: the head tracker's aim (head_tracking.md)│
   │    composed into the idle move (or held on, with focus) │
   │  emits set_target(head, antennas, body_yaw) each tick   │
   └───────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────┘
                               ▼
   ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  DAEMON                                                 │
   │  + speech wobble       (offsets, audio-reactive)        │
   │  → inverse kinematics → motors                          │
   └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **Primary moves are exclusive.** One plays at a time, sequentially, from a queue.
- **Gaze is a layer of the loop.** The head tracker ([head_tracking.md](head_tracking.md)) hands the loop an aim — the head pose that looks at the tracked face — and the loop composes it into the idle move's pose, so the robot looks at someone *and* breathes ("The gaze layer" below). The daemon's own tracking is not used to steer the head — the bridge never arms it ([robot.md](../core/robot.md)).
- **Wobbling is additive, daemon-side.** It composes with whatever target the loop is sending and never enters the loop.
- **Idle is not a special state.** It is the move that plays when the queue is empty; which move depends on the presence switch and the idle mode.

## Core concepts / Decided

### Presence and the idle mode

| `presence` | `idle` | When the queue is empty the loop… |
|---|---|---|
| on | `"breathing"` | plays `BreathingMove` — the robot visibly breathes |
| on | `"hold"` | plays `HoldMove` — a still head at the neutral pose (the target is re-sent every tick, so tracking and wobble still compose against it) |
| on | `"custom"` | plays the caller's registered idle move ("Custom idle moves" below); with none registered, `HoldMove` |
| off | *(ignored)* | emits **nothing**. The head stays where the last move ended; the bridge only commands the head while a verb runs |

- **Presence** is the background behaviour as a whole: while on, the robot never goes dead between verbs. Off is for a caller that drives the head itself through the raw robot, or wants the bridge's motion output limited to what its verbs ask for. Off does **not** stop emotions: the same loop plays them, it just goes quiet afterwards.
- **The idle mode** selects the idle move under presence: `"breathing"` (the built-in animation), `"hold"` (the still neutral), or `"custom"` (the caller's own idle move).
- Both are **modes**: each holds until changed, the defaults are presence on and idle `"breathing"` (the config's `motion` block, [config.md](../core/config.md)), and each has a verb and a read-only property on the bridge — `set_presence(enabled)` / `presence`, `set_idle(mode)` / `idle` ([bridge.md](../core/bridge.md)). Neither needs motors (the loop is paused without them, below).
- **Change semantics**, identical for both:
  - *Idle when changed* → the loop transitions at once, through the usual blend. Leaving an idle move that is playing — `"breathing"` or a custom move, for any other mode — fades its plan out to neutral first ("Leaving an idle move mid-plan" below), then the new idle move blends in from neutral: breathing starts a fresh plan whose first segment starts at rest, so there is no jump. Leaving the hold blends straight into the new idle move. Presence on blends from the present pose into the idle move. Presence **off** while idle stops commanding at once, with no easing — the caller turning it off wants the head, and stopping the stream moves nothing: the head simply holds wherever the idle move had it, up to a breath (5 mm) and a corner of the rotation envelope (10.3°) from neutral under the built-in breathing.
  - *A primary move is playing when changed* → the change is recorded and takes effect when the queue next drains. An emotion is never interrupted to change idle behaviour.
  - *Idempotent* → setting the current value is a no-op, not a re-transition.

### The loop — one writer, fixed rate

- **Only the motion loop calls `set_target`.** Nothing else in the bridge sends a target: not the bridge, not the media layer. The upstream helpers that run their own trajectory — `async_play_move` / `play_move`, `goto_target`, `wake_up`, `goto_sleep` — are **not called by the bridge** while a session is open, for two reasons verified on SDK 1.10 ([../docs/reachy-mini-api.md](../../docs/reachy-mini-api.md)): a client-side `async_play_move` is a second 100 Hz writer that would fight the loop's stream, and a daemon-side move (`goto_target`, `wake_up`, `goto_sleep`) holds a move guard for its duration during which the daemon **drops every `set_target` from every client** with a warning. The caller keeps the raw robot as an escape hatch ([robot.md](../core/robot.md)); driving the head through it while presence is on produces exactly that fight, which is what `set_presence(False)` is for.
- **A dedicated thread**, not an asyncio task: the tick must not jitter when `say` or the mic tap is busy on the event loop, and `set_target` is a blocking socket send. The bridge talks to it through a thread-safe command queue; each primary move it submits is awaited through a future the loop resolves when the move ends (or fails), so the bridge's verbs never block the event loop and cancel takes effect within one tick.
- **`CONTROL_HZ = 60`**, on a monotonic clock (`t = now − move_start`). Not 100: the conversation app's breathing at ~100 Hz produces visible micro-vibrations on the Stewart platform from the tiny per-tick deltas of a 5 mm / 0.1 Hz sine (upstream issue, open). 60 Hz is the rate its current manager runs at; whether it is the right rate on hardware is open question 1.
- **The tick.** When the current move has ended (or there is none), the loop takes the next queued primary, else the idle move for the current presence and idle mode; the new move is entered through a blend (below). It then evaluates the current move at `t`, sends `set_target(head=…, antennas=…, body_yaw=…)`, remembers that as the **last commanded target**, and sleeps out the remainder of the period. A move that returns `None` for a component leaves that component at the last commanded value.
- **Every transition is a blend; the loop never snaps.** Entering any move — a primary from idle, idle from a primary's end pose, one idle from another, a resume after a pause — goes through a `GotoMove` (upstream's, minjerk) of **`BLEND_S = 0.5` s** from the *source pose* to the new move's `evaluate(0)`, then the move plays from `t = 0`. The source pose is the last commanded target while the loop is commanding continuously; whenever it was **not** commanding — session start, a resume after the motors were off, presence turned back on — it is the **present pose read from the robot** (`get_current_head_pose()`, and antennas / body yaw from `get_current_joint_positions()`, body yaw being the first head joint), because the last target may be stale. `BreathingMove` emits absolute poses, so entering it directly from an arbitrary end-of-emotion pose is what produced the visible "head slowly descends" artefact in the conversation app; the blend removes it.
- **A history of head poses.** The loop's thread records the head pose the robot **reports** — `get_current_head_pose()`, which the SDK serves from the pose the daemon publishes on every control tick, so the read never blocks — on every pass of its 60 Hz cycle, commanding or not (paused, presence off), over the last `GAZE_HISTORY_S = 4.0` s (a ring buffer written on the motion thread, read under a lock; a read that raises is skipped). It answers `head_pose_history()` — the recorded times and poses, as arrays — and `head_pose_at(t)` for a time on the monotonic clock: the pose recorded nearest `t`, the oldest kept for a time before the window, the latest for one after it, and the pose read from the robot while nothing is recorded yet (before the thread starts). The reported pose, not the commanded one: the actual head lags the target by the IK and the motors, by an amount that differs between the sim and a robot. The head tracker ([head_tracking.md](head_tracking.md)) aims a face against the pose the head had when its frame was taken, and estimates that delay from this history.

### The gaze layer

The head tracker ([head_tracking.md](head_tracking.md)) hands the loop an **aim** — a 4×4 head pose that looks at the tracked face — and a **focus** flag, through thread-safe commands (`set_gaze(aim, focus=False)`; `aim=None` withdraws it). The commands are accepted whether or not the loop is running: sent before `start()` they wait in the session's command queue and take effect at its first tick, sent after `stop()` they are dropped — so the bridge wires the tracker to a session it has constructed but not yet started, and the tracker may outlive the session's stop by a poll. The loop composes the aim into the idle move; the tracker owns the geometry, the loop owns the timing:

- **Composition.** An `IdleMove` describes two motions ([the moves](#the-moves)): `offsets(t)`, its motion while nobody is tracked, as offsets from neutral, and `gaze_offsets(t)`, its motion while the head tracks a face, as offsets from the **aim**. Each tick the loop evaluates both at the same `t` and sends `interp(idle_pose, aim ⊕ gaze_pose, w_eff)`: the idle pose (neutral plus `offsets(t)`) toward the gaze pose composed onto the aim — its translation added in world axes, its rotation about the head's own origin, upstream's `compose_world_offset` order, the one the daemon uses for the speech offsets — with `interp` upstream's `linear_pose_interpolation`. The antennas interpolate between the two values' antennas the same way; body yaw stays neutral. So at `w = 1` the head looks at the face and does whatever the move's `gaze_offsets` says around that direction — breathing's own is its breath and antennas in full with its roaming toned down — the layer's weight `w` being the fade below, `1` once faded in. With **focus** the gaze pose's head is neutral — the head holds exactly on the aim — while its antennas stay the move's; a change of focus fades over `BLEND_S` like the weight, so it never steps the head. For the hold and for a blend (neither an `IdleMove`) the gaze pose is the aim itself. This replaces upstream's blend-then-discard, where the head went dead on the face and only the antennas stayed alive.
- **Easing.** The loop keeps its own eased aim: each tick it moves it toward the latest aim it was handed by `GAZE_ALPHA = 0.12` (upstream's 0.15 per tick at 50 Hz, scaled to 60), starting from the head's present pose the first time an aim arrives — so a new aim, arriving ten times a second, never steps the head.
- **An effective weight that fades.** The weight applied each tick eases toward its target over `BLEND_S` (minjerk): the target is `1` while an idle move plays and an aim is held, `0` otherwise — a withdrawn aim (the face lost), tracking stopped, a primary playing (including its entry blend and the exit blend at session end), the loop paused. The layer therefore **fades in** over `BLEND_S` at every idle entry with an aim and **fades out** when the aim is withdrawn — the hand-back to the idle move that the bridge used to orchestrate through daemon weights and a re-anchor — and it is **not applied to primaries**: an emotion's entry blend starts from the last commanded target, gaze included, and plays the choreography as recorded; the idle re-entry after it blends from the end pose into the idle move and the layer fades back in from there.
- **Presence off** means the loop commands nothing while idle, so the layer has nothing to compose into: tracking then has no effect on the head (detection and the face report are unaffected). A caller who turns presence off to drive the head through the raw robot is not fought by the gaze.

### The moves

All are upstream `reachy_mini.motion.move.Move` subclasses — `duration` plus `evaluate(t) -> (head_4x4, antennas_rad, body_yaw_rad)` — so the loop treats a recorded emotion, a blend and an idle move identically.

- **`IdleOffsets`** — the vocabulary of every animated idle move: a frozen dataclass holding the pose's signed **offsets from neutral in human units** at one instant — `z_mm` (head height), `roll_deg` / `pitch_deg` / `yaw_deg` (head rotation), `antenna_right_deg` / `antenna_left_deg` (each antenna's lean **outward** beyond its neutral lean; negative leans inward). Every field defaults to `0.0`, so `IdleOffsets()` is neutral. `pose(scale=1.0)` is the single place offsets become a pose: the head from upstream's `create_head_pose` (z and the three angles, each multiplied by `scale`), the antennas as `NEUTRAL_ANTENNAS + ANTENNA_OUTWARD × radians(scale × lean)`, body yaw `0.0`. At scale `1` it is the move's pose, at scale `0` it is exactly neutral, and the values between are what the fade-out below rides out on. The head's x and y translation and the body yaw stay neutral under every idle move.
- **`IdleMove`** — the base class of every animated idle move: an upstream `Move` of infinite `duration` whose one abstract method is `offsets(t) -> IdleOffsets`, the motion while nobody is tracked; `evaluate(t)` is `offsets(t).pose()`. It has one optional method, **`gaze_offsets(t) -> IdleOffsets`** — the motion while the head tracks a face, as offsets from the aim rather than from neutral ("The gaze layer" above). Not overridden, it returns `IdleOffsets()`: **the move does nothing on the body during gaze**, the head sits exactly on the aim and the antennas rest. An author overrides it with a dedicated behaviour, or with the main behaviour scaled — `return self.offsets(t).scaled(rotation=0.25)` — through the helper **`IdleOffsets.scaled(*, translation=1.0, rotation=1.0, antennas=1.0)`**, which returns a copy with the z, the three angles and the two antenna leans multiplied by their factor. Both methods take the same `t`, so a move that alternates between them as faces come and go stays consistent with its own plan. `BreathingMove` is the built-in one, and a caller's custom idle move is another ("Custom idle moves" below). Because every `IdleMove` is described as offsets, the loop can fade any of them out to neutral at rest.

- **Neutral** is upstream's `INIT_HEAD_POSE` (the identity, the pose `wake_up` ends at), `INIT_ANTENNAS_JOINT_POSITIONS` (`[-0.1745, 0.1745]` rad — ~10° apart on purpose, upstream's "reduce shaking at vertical"; not `[0, 0]`) and body yaw `0.0`. The constants come from `reachy_mini.reachy_mini` (SDK 1.10).
- **`BreathingMove`** — infinite duration; the idle move that reads as alive. It is a **randomised plan** of six independent scalar tracks — the head's z offset, its roll, pitch and yaw, and the two antennas — each a sequence of segments that **start and end at rest** (zero velocity), composed into one pose at every `evaluate`. The move takes a seedable random source (`random.Random`) at construction: the loop builds a fresh, unseeded move at each idle entry, tests pass a seed. A plan is generated lazily as `t` grows and never changes once generated, so `evaluate(t)` stays a pure function of `t` for a given seed, as the `Move` contract expects. It is an `IdleMove`: `offsets(t)` returns the six tracks' values as an `IdleOffsets` (the tracks run in metres and radians and are converted at that boundary), and `evaluate(t)` is that value's pose at scale `1`. Its `gaze_offsets(t)` is `offsets(t).scaled(rotation=BREATHING_GAZE_ROTATION_SCALE)`, `BREATHING_GAZE_ROTATION_SCALE = 0.25`: while the head holds a face it keeps breathing and its antennas keep roaming and flicking in full, but the head's roaming — ±8° of yaw would carry it off the person — is toned down to a quarter, which reads as attentive liveliness rather than looking away.
  - *Breath track (head z):* alternates a **breath** and a **rest**. A breath is a raised-cosine rise-and-fall on the **z axis** — `z = BREATH_Z_M · (1 − cos(2π t / BREATH_S)) / 2`, peaking `BREATH_Z_M = 5 mm` above neutral over `BREATH_S = 5 s` — followed by a rest at neutral of a random `BREATH_REST_S = 1 to 5 s` (uniform). The track begins with a breath, so a fresh idle shows life at once. The head rests *at* neutral (the wake-up pose) and rises from it, never dipping below. The rests are what make the breathing organic: a robot that breathes on a fixed cycle reads as a machine.
  - *Head rotation tracks (roll, pitch, yaw):* the idle head looks about. Each axis, independently, alternates a **hold** of random `HEAD_HOLD_S = 1.2 to 4.5 s` and a **minjerk move** of random `HEAD_MOVE_S = 1.2 to 2.8 s` to a new angle within its own limit — `HEAD_YAW_RAD = 8°`, `HEAD_PITCH_RAD = 5°`, `HEAD_ROLL_RAD = 4°` either side of neutral. Each track begins with a hold at neutral. The new angle is drawn **at least `ROAM_MIN_TRAVEL_FRACTION = 0.4` of the axis' full span away from where the track sits** — uniform over the axis' range with the band around the current value removed — so no move is too small to see; a plain uniform draw would often land next to the current angle and spend a second and a half travelling two degrees. Three axes on separate streams put at least one of them in motion about 70 % of the time, with the head sitting ~6° off neutral on average and 10.3° at the corner of the envelope. The head's **translation is the breath track alone**: x, y and body yaw stay neutral, so the idle turns and tilts the head without sliding it about.
  - *Antenna tracks:* each antenna, independently, alternates a **hold** of random `ANTENNA_HOLD_S = 0.4 to 2.5 s` with one of two shapes, a **move** or a **flick**, drawn `ANTENNA_FLICK_PROBABILITY = 0.35` of the time for the flick. Each track begins with a hold at the floor.
    - A **move** is a minjerk travel (upstream's `time_trajectory`, `MIN_JERK`) to a new angle in `ANTENNA_MIN_RAD .. ANTENNA_MAX_RAD` = **10° to 25° outward** from vertical, drawn by the same minimum-travel rule as the head's rotations. Its **duration follows from its travel**: an average speed is drawn in `ANTENNA_SPEED_RAD_S = 20 to 70 °/s` and the duration is `travel / speed`, clamped to `ANTENNA_MOVE_S = 0.25 to 1.2 s`. Drawing the speed rather than the duration is what keeps a long sweep from crawling: a fixed duration makes every move as slow as the longest one, which is what a plain `uniform(0.8, 2.5)` produced.
    - A **flick** is a raised-cosine out-and-back from where the track sits — `ANTENNA_FLICK_S = 0.25 to 0.5 s`, peaking `ANTENNA_FLICK_RAD = 12° to 25°` further out, capped at `ANTENNA_FLICK_MAX_RAD = 45°` outward. It is the quick perk that punctuates the slow roaming and carries most of the idle's expressiveness; it returns to where it started, so it is rest-to-rest like everything else and needs no new machinery — it is the breath's shape on an antenna. Only a flick reaches past `ANTENNA_MAX_RAD`: the sustained roaming window stays 10°–25°.
    - The resulting motion is in the band the emotions library itself commands: the antennas' speed reads ~40 °/s at the 90th percentile and ~160 °/s at the 99th, against the library's 128 °/s and 305 °/s, and against the 4 °/s and 13 °/s of a fixed-duration plan over the same window.
    - The two antennas draw from separate streams of the random source, so their motion is asymmetric and uncorrelated — and because both only lean outward from vertical, they can never meet. "Outward" in joint terms is the sign of upstream's sleep pose (`SLEEP_ANTENNAS_JOINT_POSITIONS = [-3.05, 3.05]`, the antennas folded fully out): **negative for the right antenna, positive for the left** — `ANTENNA_OUTWARD = [-1, +1]`, joint value = sign × angle. The floor is the neutral 10° lean, not true vertical: upstream chose that offset to keep the antennas from shaking at vertical, and it makes the floor exactly `NEUTRAL_ANTENNAS`.
  - Body yaw `0.0`.
  - `evaluate(0)` is exactly neutral **at rest** — every track's first segment is a hold at its neutral value, save the breath's, whose raised cosine is flat at `t = 0` — and every segment boundary is at rest, so the entry blend (minjerk, ending at zero velocity) hands off with continuous velocity and the move needs no entry envelope. A sine has no such property — its value is zero exactly where its slope is steepest — which is why the idle is built from rest-to-rest segments rather than a continuous oscillation.
- **Leaving an idle move mid-plan** — a change of idle mode away from a playing `IdleMove` (`"breathing"` or a custom move), a new custom move registered over a playing one, or session exit while one plays — goes through a **fade-out** rather than a plain blend: the same move keeps playing from the moment of interruption while a minjerk-shaped envelope scales every offset from neutral down to zero over `BLEND_S` (the `IdleOffsets` value's `scale` factor, so the rotations fade with everything else — a linear scaling of the Euler angles, indistinguishable from a geodesic one at the built-in envelope's ≤ 8°), landing exactly at neutral with zero velocity before whatever follows (the next idle move's blend, or nothing) takes over. A plain blend assumes its source is at rest, and a track caught mid-segment is not. An interruption that lands in a rest is the same fade over an offset that is already zero. Presence off while an idle move plays stops dead by design (above).
- **`HoldMove`** — infinite duration; constant neutral. A plain `Move`: it has no offsets to fade, so leaving it is a plain blend from neutral.
- **Blends** are upstream `GotoMove` instances built by the loop (start pose → target pose, `BLEND_S`, minjerk).
- **Recorded emotions** are upstream `RecordedMove`s from the emotions library, resolved by the bridge as today ([bridge.md](../core/bridge.md) "Emotions library"); on the `fake` backend the bridge's offline stub library yields real `Move`s with a short trajectory (a small nod, 0.3 s), so the loop path runs in `tests/`.

### Custom idle moves

A caller replaces the built-in breathing with their own idle animation by subclassing `IdleMove` and registering a **factory** for it; the loop plays it whenever the idle mode is `"custom"`.

```python
class SlowNod(IdleMove):
    def offsets(self, t: float) -> IdleOffsets:
        return IdleOffsets(pitch_deg=4.0 * (1.0 - math.cos(2.0 * math.pi * t / 6.0)) / 2.0)

config = ReachyMiniConfig(motion=MotionSettings(idle="custom", idle_move=SlowNod))
# or, while entered:  await bridge.set_idle_move(SlowNod); await bridge.set_idle("custom")
```

- **A factory, called at every idle entry.** The registered value is a zero-argument callable returning a fresh `IdleMove` — a subclass itself (`SlowNod`), or any function building one. The loop calls it each time the custom idle move is entered (session start, after every emotion, after a mode change, a resume), exactly as it builds a fresh `BreathingMove` each time — so `t` starts at `0` at every entry, and a randomised move draws a new plan each time.
- **The mode and the registered move are independent.** The factory is stored whatever the idle mode is, and survives mode changes; it plays whenever the mode is `"custom"`. With the mode `"custom"` and nothing registered, the loop plays `HoldMove`. Registering `None` clears it.
- **Registering while the custom idle plays** takes effect at once: a playing custom move fades out to neutral, then the new one blends in; over the hold, the new one blends straight in. Registering always re-enters the custom idle, including with the factory already registered. During a primary it applies at the next idle entry; in any other mode it is only stored.
- **Three places carry it, one flow.** The config's `motion.idle_move` field (a Python-only field: a JSON file names the mode, code supplies the move — [config.md](../core/config.md)), the bridge's `set_idle_move(factory)` verb and `idle_move` property ([bridge.md](../core/bridge.md)), and `MotionSession(robot, presence=…, idle=…, idle_move=…)`, which the bridge builds from the first two.
- **What the author guarantees.** `offsets(t)` — and `gaze_offsets(t)` when overridden — runs on the motion thread at 60 Hz: it returns promptly, never blocks, and never touches the robot or the bridge (the loop is the one writer). It is a pure function of `t` for a given instance, continuous, and **starts at rest** — zero velocity at `t = 0` — because the entry blend ends at rest at `offsets(0)`; `offsets(0)` itself may be any pose. Amplitude is the author's: the loop checks that every offset is finite, and upstream's SDK clamps joint ranges.
- **Under gaze, the move is motion around the person.** `gaze_offsets(t)` is composed onto the aim, so whatever it returns happens *around wherever the head is looking*: a nod stays a nod at the person, a yaw sweep becomes a sweep around them. Tracking wins by construction — an idle move cannot look away from a tracked face; an app that wants the idle to own the head stops tracking; one that wants the head still on the person sets `focus`. A move that should be still while someone is watched simply leaves `gaze_offsets` alone.
- **Checked at registration.** `MotionSession` — at construction and in `set_idle_move` — calls the factory once on the caller's thread and raises `ValueError` when the value is not callable, the call raises, the result is not an `IdleMove`, or its `offsets(0.0)` or `gaze_offsets(0.0)` is not an `IdleOffsets` of finite numbers. A rejected factory changes nothing: the one registered before stays.
- **A custom move that fails while playing falls back to the hold.** When the factory, `offsets(t)` or `gaze_offsets(t)` raises on the motion thread, or returns something other than an `IdleOffsets` of finite numbers, the loop logs **one** `WARNING` (`custom idle move failed; holding neutral until another is registered`), marks the registered move failed, and plays `HoldMove` wherever it would have played the custom move — so a broken move costs one log line, not sixty a second. Registering a move (the same factory included) clears the mark. A failure during the session-exit fade-out stops the loop at once.

### Emotions through the loop

`play_emotion(name)` ([bridge.md](../core/bridge.md)) resolves the move, then hands it to the loop as a primary; the loop plays it — this is the orchestration:

1. **Queue.** Primaries are FIFO. A second `play_emotion` while one plays waits its turn; cancelling a waiting one removes it from the queue.
2. **Enter.** When the move becomes current, the loop blends from the source pose to `move.evaluate(0)` over `BLEND_S` (this replaces upstream's `initial_goto_duration`, which covers only the entry and would be a daemon-side move anyway).
3. **Sound.** As the trajectory starts (after the blend, as upstream orders it), the loop starts the move's sidecar sound through `media.play_sound(str(move.sound_path))` when the move has one.
4. **Play** the trajectory for `move.duration`, evaluating at the loop rate.
5. **Complete.** The verb's future resolves when the trajectory ends. The return to neutral is **not** part of the verb: what follows is the idle behaviour's business — with presence on the idle move blends in from the end pose (so an emotion ends, eases back to neutral and breathing resumes, with nothing to queue); with presence off the head stays at the recording's end pose. Two emotions queued back-to-back blend directly from the first's end pose into the second, with no detour through neutral.
6. **Cancel / failure.** Cancelling the awaiting task (or the move raising) drops the primary at the next tick: the loop stops commanding the trajectory and, when it had started the move's sound, stops it through the `stop_sound` callable the bridge hands it ([audio.md](../audio/audio.md)'s `MediaSession.stop_sound()`, which also resets the wobbler) — the party that started the sound is the one that stops it, so a primary cancelled while it waits in the queue stops nothing and the emotion playing ahead of it plays on. The loop acknowledges the drop (the primary's `dropped` event), which `play_emotion` waits for before re-raising, so the effect has stopped when the `CancelledError` reaches the caller; the loop then behaves as at step 5 from where the cancel caught it. This keeps [bridge.md](../core/bridge.md)'s "Cancellation" guarantees: prompt, effect stopped, session usable.

Around the move, the loop leaves the gaze layer out ("The gaze layer" above: the layer is not applied to a primary, and fades back in at the idle re-entry after it), and the bridge adjusts the one daemon-side layer and **restores it afterwards, on every exit path** (completion, cancel, failure):

- **Wobbling is paused for the move.** If wobbling is on, the bridge calls `disable_wobbling()` before the first `play_emotion` in flight begins and `enable_wobbling()` when the last one ends — one pause across consecutive primaries, counted as a lease per in-flight `play_emotion`, taken before the disable call is awaited so a cancel caught inside that call still releases it — the cancel returns at once, the call completes in its thread, and the restore follows its completion; the release restores the bridge's *current* record, so a caller's `set_wobbling(False)` during an emotion stays off. Library emotions carry their own sound; letting it drive the wobbler as well puts audio-driven motion on top of the choreographed motion on the same head, which reads as twitchy. The emotion's sound still plays. A `say` running concurrently with an emotion therefore does not sway the head for that emotion's duration. This resolves [bridge.md](../core/bridge.md)'s former open question on wobbling during `play_emotion`.

### Interactions between the layers

| Combination | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| wobble + idle move (breathing / hold / custom) | additive — the robot reacts to audio while idle, still or animated |
| wobble + emotion | paused by the bridge for the move (above) |
| gaze + idle move (breathing / hold / custom) | composed — the idle move plays *around* the aim: the head looks at the face and breathes, roams and flicks its antennas (with focus, the head holds on the face and only the antennas move); once nobody has been seen for the tracker's loss timeout the aim is withdrawn and the layer fades out ([head_tracking.md](head_tracking.md)), so the robot idles in full |
| gaze + emotion | not applied — the emotion plays as recorded; the layer fades back in at the idle re-entry after it |
| gaze + wobble | additive — the daemon's speech offsets ride on the composed pose |
| emotion + emotion | exclusive — queued, never overlapped |
| breathing + hold + custom | exclusive — one idle move, chosen by the idle mode |
| presence off + wobble | unaffected — the daemon-side mode stays on; a non-breathing robot still reacts to audio against its last target |
| presence off + gaze | no effect on the head — the loop commands nothing while idle; detection and the face report continue |

### Motors

The loop **pauses while the motors are not `enabled`** and sends no target at all: targets into limp or gravity-compensated motors do nothing, and upstream's `enable_motors()` pins the targets to the *present* pose before switching torque on, so a stream that kept running would snap the head from wherever it drooped to the idle pose the instant torque returned. Pause and resume are driven by the bridge, not by polling:

- at session start the bridge reads the motor state once and starts the loop paused unless it is `enabled`;
- `set_motors_state("enabled")` resumes it, `"disabled"` / `"gravity_compensation"` pause it — pausing drops the queue and fails an in-flight primary with `BridgeError`, so a `play_emotion` cut short by a rest reports it rather than hanging;
- `play_emotion` resumes it (its motor precondition has just passed — this also covers a daemon that enabled the motors itself, e.g. its boot wake-up, which the bridge never saw).

Every resume **re-anchors**: the first blend starts from the present pose read from the robot. A daemon-side change the bridge did not make (an idle-timeout `goto_sleep` on a shared robot disables torque; its own `wake_up` ends at neutral with torque on) is harmless either way: targets into disabled motors do nothing, and the next api-driven resume re-anchors.

The verbs that move the robot keep their fail-fast precondition ([bridge.md](../core/bridge.md) "Motors"): `play_emotion` raises `MotorsNotEnabledError` rather than queueing into a paused loop.

### Lifecycle

The loop is a **`MotionSession`** with the `async start()` / `async stop()` lifecycle every session of the bridge has ([bridge.md](../core/bridge.md) "Lifecycle"), started by `ReachyMiniBridge.start()` after the media session and the wobbling setup, and stopped first:

- **`start()`:** start the thread with the config's `motion.presence` / `motion.idle` / `motion.idle_move`, paused unless the motors read `enabled`. A registered `idle_move` is checked here ("Custom idle moves"), so a bad one fails bring-up with `ValueError`. Nothing moves until the first tick's blend.
- **`stop()`:** when presence is on and the loop is commanding, ease the head to the neutral hold (one `BLEND_S` stage, awaited — the fade-out when an `IdleMove` is playing, a plain blend otherwise) so the robot is left standing neutral for the next app, then stop the thread — the join runs off the event loop (`asyncio.to_thread`), never on it. With presence off, or the loop paused, just stop. Pending primaries are dropped and their futures cancelled. The daemon's own idle reset is not involved: it fires only when a *managed* app slot (a WebRTC session or an `AppManager` app) goes free, and SDK clients like the bridge bypass that lock entirely ([../docs/reachy-mini-api.md](../../docs/reachy-mini-api.md)) — so the bridge parks the robot itself.
- **Bring-up cancel / failure** unwinds it like every other step (the bridge's `AsyncExitStack`).
- The thread never outlives the session. An exception raised inside a tick (a bad pose, a failed send on a live connection) is logged, fails the in-flight primary's future when there is one, and drops the current move; the loop keeps running and re-selects on its next tick, so one bad move never kills the idle behaviour.
- **A lost connection is not a bad tick.** When `set_target` raises because the daemon is gone — upstream's builtin `ConnectionError("Lost connection with the server.")` once its client's receive loop has noticed the close, or `websockets.exceptions.ConnectionClosed` (not a `ConnectionError`) on the send that races the close — the loop logs **one** `WARNING` (`motion loop paused: lost connection to the daemon`), fails the in-flight primary and every queued one with a `BridgeError` chained to the upstream error, and **pauses** as after `pause()`: it sends nothing more, so nothing more is logged. The pause is permanent for the session: a later `submit` fails its future with the same `BridgeError` at once instead of un-pausing the loop, `resume()` is a no-op, and `stop()` skips the exit blend (there is no live socket to blend into) and returns promptly. Recovery is out of scope — upstream's client does not reconnect, so a lost connection ends the session; the caller exits and re-enters the bridge. Without this, a daemon that dies mid-session (USB unplugged, a crash, someone stopping a borrowed daemon) produces one warning per tick — sixty lines a second — until the app exits; the Ctrl+C case itself is removed at the source by [daemon.md](../daemon/daemon.md) "The child runs in its own session".

### `fake` backend support

`FakeReachyMini` ([robot.md](../core/robot.md)) gains the loop's consumed members: `set_target(head, antennas, body_yaw)` recorded on a dedicated `targets` list (not `commands`, which a 60 Hz stream would swamp) with the most recent kept as `last_target`; `get_current_head_pose()` and `get_current_joint_positions()` returning the last commanded values (identity / neutral before any), so the fake is a trivially consistent robot for re-anchoring. `async_play_move` leaves the consumed slice: the bridge no longer calls it. The offline emotions library yields moves with a real short trajectory. The loop itself runs unchanged on the fake, at real time, so `tests/` observe its stream: a breathing robot's recorded head targets rise from neutral and return to it, resting at neutral between breaths, and its antennas roam independently within their outward range; a hold sends a constant neutral; a custom idle move's offsets show in the recorded targets; presence off sends nothing once idle. Tests of the plan itself seed the move and evaluate it at chosen times, with no waiting.

## Relationship to the other specs

- **[bridge.md](../core/bridge.md):** `play_emotion` runs through this loop (and pauses wobbling around the move); `set_presence` / `set_idle` / `set_idle_move` and their properties are the modes' verbs; `set_motors_state` pauses / resumes the loop; the session enters last and exits first.
- **[head_tracking.md](head_tracking.md):** the head tracker hands the loop its aim and focus (`set_gaze`) and reads the head-pose history (`head_pose_history`); the loop composes, eases and fades ("The gaze layer").
- **[camera.md](../vision/camera.md):** the camera feed stamps each frame with `head_pose_at(ts)` — the session object is constructed before the feed starts, and answers with the robot's reported pose until the loop's thread records its own.
- **[config.md](../core/config.md):** the `motion` block (`presence`, `idle`, and the Python-only `idle_move`) sets the modes' initial values.
- **[robot.md](../core/robot.md):** the consumed slice grows `set_target` and the two pose readers, and drops `async_play_move`; the fake records targets.
- **[audio.md](../audio/audio.md):** the move's sound starts through `media.play_sound` and stops through `MediaSession.stop_sound()`; wobbling is paused around an emotion.
- **[testing.md](../testing/testing.md):** smoothness and drift cannot be asserted by a test; the live tier asserts what it can (breathing moves z; an emotion returns to neutral; a cancel stops it) and the implementation plan carries the on-robot checks.

## Open questions

1. **Tick rate on hardware.** `CONTROL_HZ = 60` and `BLEND_S = 0.5` are the values to start from. On the Reachy Mini Lite (SDK 1.10), 50 Hz is worse, not better: a per-tick step is bigger at a slower rate, and any velocity discontinuity at a handoff shows through the whole body. What keeps the stream snap-free is therefore the handoff discipline above — every idle segment boundary at rest, a fade-out on interruption — not the rate. Still open: whether residual micro-vibration remains at 60 Hz once the handoffs are clean (amplitude / deadband tuning), whether slow downward drift shows over a long idle, and the deferred case of a primary (an emotion) preempting an idle move mid-segment, which hands off through a plain `blend_into` from the momentary pose — the same nonzero-velocity gap the fade-out closes on exit, not yet observed as a problem.
2. **Manual moves.** `goto`-style primaries (`set_head_pose`, `nod`, `look_at`) slot into the queue as `GotoMove`s / composed moves once [bridge.md](../core/bridge.md)'s deferred manual verbs land; the loop needs no change for them.
3. **Listening antenna freeze.** The conversation app freezes the antennas while it listens (and blends them back over 0.4 s) as a "listening" cue. Whether the bridge offers such a cue is deferred; it would be another idle mode or an overlay on the idle move.
4. **Rate-limiting non-connection tick faults.** A *persistent* fault that is not a lost connection (a move whose `evaluate` keeps raising, say) still logs once per tick under the "Lifecycle" contract. Logging the first and then a count every few seconds is deferred: no such flood has been observed, and a failing move is dropped on its first bad tick, so the idle move that follows is a fresh one.
5. **An amplitude envelope for custom idle moves.** The loop checks a custom move's offsets for finiteness only; upstream's SDK clamps joint ranges. Whether the bridge also clamps custom offsets to an idle-sized envelope is deferred until a custom move on hardware shows the need.
6. **Naming a custom idle move from a config file.** `motion.idle_move` is a Python-only field. Whether a JSON config may also name a factory by import path (`"package.module:SlowNod"`) is deferred until a deployment needs it.
7. **Gaze tuning on hardware.** `GAZE_ALPHA` and the `BLEND_S` fade of the effective weight are starting values chosen to match upstream's daemon-side easing, and breathing's `BREATHING_GAZE_ROTATION_SCALE` of a quarter is a first guess at how much roaming still reads as attention rather than distraction; all three are to be judged on a robot with a person in front of it.
