# MuJoCo 3.3.x declared directly, and the camera stream shown over the MuJoCo viewer

**Status:** Done — built and green on the offline gate, and seen live through the control panel on 2026-09-25: the webcam picture (mirrored, on request), then the rendered eye camera's (once it got its frame and label — unframed, it was indistinguishable from the scene behind it), with clean daemon stops in between. The window-resize behaviour is pinned by the offline test on a fake viewer handle only.

Implements the settled behavior in `specs/project.md` ("Optional extras" — the `sim` extra requires MuJoCo itself) and the design to be written into `specs/daemon/sim_daemon.md` ("Viewer overlay"), `specs/core/config.md` (`daemon.sim_displays`) and `specs/daemon/daemon.md` ("display flags"): the bridge's `sim` extra takes the newest MuJoCo 3.3.x instead of upstream's pinned 3.3.0, and a `daemon.sim_displays.camera_overlay` config field — the first of a `sim_displays` block meant to grow more viewer displays — makes the sim daemon draw its camera stream — the webcam, or the rendered eye camera — as a picture in a corner of the MuJoCo viewer window, so a person watching the sim sees what the robot sees while it follows a face. It deliberately leaves out any in-scene screen (a textured prop the eye camera could see), calibration of the overlay to the robot's gaze, and any change to the camera stream clients receive.

## Why

The MuJoCo passive viewer can draw an RGB image over its 3D view on every frame (`viewer.Handle.set_images`, with `set_texts` for a label) since MuJoCo 3.3.1 (April 2025). Upstream's `mujoco` extra is the single requirement `mujoco==3.3.0`, an exact pin from February 2025 that nothing depending on `reachy_mini` can loosen: pip constraints only narrow, and uv's `override-dependencies` is honoured for the root project only — measured on 2026-09-25, a downstream project depending on `reachy-mini-bridge[sim]` resolved 3.3.0 while the bridge's own `pyproject.toml` carried the override, and 3.3.7 once the `sim` extra required `mujoco` directly. The report drafted for upstream is [../docs/upstream-mujoco-version-pin.md](../docs/upstream-mujoco-version-pin.md).

Without an overlay, the only way to see what the simulated robot's camera sees is a client pulling frames (`get_camera_frame()`) into a window of its own; a person testing face tracking by hand in front of the webcam has the viewer window open already, and that is where the feed belongs.

## Measurements behind the design

- **The overlay API** (read on the tagged sources of `python/mujoco/viewer.py` at 3.3.0, 3.3.1, 3.3.4, 3.3.6, 3.4.0 and `main`; identical from 3.3.1 on): `set_images` takes `(MjrRect, image)` pairs; the image must be RGB `uint8` of exactly the rectangle's `(height, width)` (a `ValueError` otherwise), top-down — the binding flips it vertically itself before `mjr_drawPixels`; the bytes are copied on the call; the images stay drawn on every frame until replaced or `clear_images`. The call waits until the render thread has taken the previous request (`WaitForAtomicNoGil(newimagerequest)`), so it blocks for up to one UI frame and must never run on the physics thread. `Handle.viewport` is the current 3D view rectangle (`uistate.rect[3]`) in framebuffer pixels — twice the window's points on a Retina display.
- **Upstream's daemon on newer MuJoCo:** `scene.xml` compiles on 3.14.0 (170 geoms, 2 cameras); the offline suite (411 tests, including the corrected backend stepped through real `mj_step`s until the head converges on a face) passes on 3.3.7 and 3.14.0; the headless e2e tier gives the same result on 3.3.0, 3.3.7 and 3.14.0 (13 passed, 6 capability skips, and the one antenna-return check that is flaky on all three alike).
- **Where the frames are:** the webcam relay's pipeline ends in `rtpvrawpay ! udpsink`, so the daemon process never holds a frame as an array; its pad probe on the payloader only timestamps. Upstream's `rendering_loop` holds each rendered frame (`im`, 1280×720 RGB) between `offscreen_renderer.render()` and `frame_sender.send_frame(im)`, and gets that renderer from `self._get_renderer(camera_name)`. The daemon has no OpenCV; GStreamer's `videoscale` and numpy indexing are the resizers available.
- **The viewer handle** is a local variable of upstream's `MujocoBackend.run()` (`viewer = mujoco.viewer.launch_passive(...)`, looked up on the `mujoco.viewer` module at call time) and `run()` calls `viewer.close()` itself after the loop; a `set_images` issued after `close()` could wait on a render thread that is gone.

1. **The specs.** `specs/daemon/sim_daemon.md` gains a "Viewer overlay" section stating the design fixed by steps 2–8 (the field, the flag, the placement and size, the two frame feeds, the latest-frame slot and its own thread, the handle capture, the close ordering, the degrade rule) and the "Testable without a daemon" bullets of the Verification below; its usage line becomes `[--camera sim|webcam] [--sim-display camera_overlay]... Both index rows follow.

2. **Config.** A new `SimDisplaySettings` dataclass (`camera_overlay: bool = False`, with the same `from_dict` / `from_json` / `from_json_file` trio as `SimCameraSettings`; `_reject_unknown_keys(block, "daemon.sim_displays", {"camera_overlay"})`; a non-boolean is `ConfigError("'daemon.sim_displays.camera_overlay' must be a boolean")`) and an `enabled()` method returning the names of the displays that are `true`, in declaration order — the one place a future display is added. `DaemonConfig.sim_displays: SimDisplaySettings = field(default_factory=SimDisplaySettings)`, accepted key `sim_displays`; `from_dict` raises `ConfigError("'daemon.sim_displays.camera_overlay' needs the viewer: set 'daemon.headless' to false")` naming the first enabled display when any is enabled and `headless` is `true` (the default, so a display only ever works with an explicit `headless: false`). Like `headless`, `scene` and `camera`, the block is a MuJoCo knob accepted and ignored on `real`. `config.example.json` gains `"sim_displays": {"camera_overlay": true}` — the example is the sim viewer seeing through the webcam, and the overlay is part of "the configuration that shows the most"; the control panel starts from it and so shows the feed.

3. **Launch command.** A `_display_flags(config)` beside `_camera_flags` emits `--sim-display <name>` for each name in `config.sim_displays.enabled()`, appended after the camera flags in every `sim` recipe (viewer and scene-file alike). Nothing else in `daemon.py` changes: the config already guarantees the viewer.

4. **Launcher argv.** `_parser` gains `--sim-display` (`action="append"`, `choices=SIM_DISPLAYS` — a module tuple holding `"camera_overlay"` today, "a viewer display to turn on; repeatable"); `run_sim_daemon` calls `parser.error("--sim-display needs the viewer (drop --headless)")` when any is given with `--headless`. A `_Displays` dataclass (`camera_overlay: bool = False`) is built from the list and passed to `corrected_backend(..., displays=...)`; the `INFO` line at start names the enabled displays.

5. **Geometry and resampling, pure functions.** `overlay_rect(viewport, fraction=OVERLAY_FRACTION, margin=OVERLAY_MARGIN)` returns the `(left, bottom, width, height)` of a 16:9 rectangle whose width is `fraction` (1/4) of the viewport's width, both sides rounded down to even numbers, placed in the viewport's top-right corner inset by `margin` (1/50 of the viewport's width) — MuJoCo rectangles have their origin at the bottom-left, so `bottom = viewport.bottom + viewport.height - height - margin`; a viewport too small for the rectangle (width or height under 32 px) yields `None` and nothing is drawn. `resample_nearest(frame, size)` returns the frame at `(height, width)` by nearest-neighbour index arrays (`frame[iy[:, None], ix]`), a no-op copy at equal size — no OpenCV. Both take plain tuples so the tests need no MuJoCo.

6. **`ViewerOverlay`.** Owns a latest-frame slot (one array reference and a `threading.Event`, replaced without blocking by `show(frame)` from any feed thread — a feed faster than the viewer only ever loses intermediate frames) and a daemon thread started by `attach(handle)`. The thread loops until stopped: wait for the event, take the frame, read `handle.viewport`, compute `overlay_rect`, resample the frame to it and call `handle.set_images([(MjrRect(...), image)])`; `viewport` is re-read on every frame so a window resize keeps the picture in its corner. `label(text)` sets a one-line `set_texts` overlay (`mjFONT_NORMAL`, `mjGRID_TOPRIGHT`) when the handle has `set_texts` — the relay calls it with the camera's name and negotiated size, the same words it already logs. `stop()` sets the flag, joins the thread, and calls `clear_images` / `clear_texts` when the handle still reports `is_running()`. **Degrade rule:** `attach` on a handle without `set_images` logs one `WARNING` — "camera overlay: needs MuJoCo 3.3.1 or later (installed X); not drawn" — and does nothing else; every other method becomes a no-op. The attribute check is the only version test.

7. **The frame feeds.**
   - *Webcam.* `relay_pipeline_description(source, source_caps, overlay=False)`: with `overlay`, the RGB 1280×720 caps are followed by `tee name=t ! queue leaky=downstream max-size-buffers=2 ! rtpvrawpay ... ! udpsink ...` (the stream branch, byte-for-byte today's tail) and a second branch `t. ! queue leaky=downstream max-size-buffers=1 ! videoscale ! video/x-raw,width=640,height=360 ! appsink name=overlay emit-signals=true max-buffers=1 drop=true sync=false` (`OVERLAY_SOURCE_SIZE`; GStreamer does the downscale, the leaky one-buffer queue keeps the stream branch from ever waiting on the overlay); without `overlay` the description is unchanged from today. `_GstPipeline` takes an optional `on_overlay_frame` and, when the pipeline has an `overlay` element, connects its `new-sample` signal to a handler that maps the buffer, copies it into a `(360, 640, 3)` `uint8` array and hands it over. `WebcamRelay` takes `overlay: ViewerOverlay | None`, passes the flag into `relay_pipeline_candidates` and the callback into `_open`, and calls `overlay.label(...)` from `_report_camera`.
   - *Rendered eye camera.* `corrected_backend` overrides `_get_renderer(camera_name)` when `displays.camera_overlay` is on and the source is `sim`: it returns a `_TappedRenderer` proxy wrapping upstream's `mujoco.Renderer` — `update_scene` forwarded, `render()` forwarded and its result handed to `overlay.show(frame[::2, ::2])` (1280×720 → 640×360 by striding, on the render thread, before the frame goes to the RTP sender). Upstream's `rendering_loop` is otherwise untouched.

8. **The viewer handle, and stopping in the right order.** With `displays.camera_overlay` on, the subclass's `run()` (now defined for both sources) replaces `mujoco.viewer.launch_passive` on the `mujoco.viewer` module for the duration of `super().run()` with a wrapper that calls the original, hands the returned handle to `overlay.attach`, binds the handle's `close` to an instance attribute that runs `overlay.stop()` first and then the original `close` — so the overlay thread has stopped before upstream's `run()` closes the viewer — and returns the handle. The original is restored in a `finally`, alongside the webcam relay's stop; `overlay.stop()` runs there too, for the case where the viewer never opened. The substitution is process-local to the daemon, the same technique as `install_tracker_intrinsics`, and goes away the day upstream keeps the handle on `self`.

9. **Docs and the map.** `docs/running-the-sim-daemon.md` ("Headfull / viewer sim" and "You in front of the sim") show `--sim-display camera_overlay` and the config field; the README's "The simulator" bullet list gets one bullet; the `AGENTS.md` project-map row for `sim_daemon.py` mentions the viewer overlay.
